# Photosynthesis & Cellular Respiration — Demo Notes for AdaptQuiz

## Photosynthesis (overview)
Photosynthesis converts light energy into chemical energy stored as glucose.
It occurs in the chloroplasts of plant cells, specifically in the thylakoid
membranes (light-dependent reactions) and the stroma (Calvin cycle).
Overall equation: 6CO2 + 6H2O + light energy -> C6H12O6 + 6O2.

## Light-dependent reactions
Light-dependent reactions take place in the thylakoid membranes. Chlorophyll
absorbs photons, splitting water (photolysis) to release oxygen, protons, and
electrons. The electron transport chain pumps protons into the thylakoid lumen,
creating a gradient that ATP synthase uses to make ATP. NADP+ is reduced to
NADPH. These reactions require light and produce ATP, NADPH, and O2.

## Calvin cycle (light-independent reactions)
The Calvin cycle runs in the stroma and does not directly require light, but it
depends on ATP and NADPH from the light reactions. Carbon fixation: the enzyme
RuBisCO attaches CO2 to ribulose-1,5-bisphosphate (RuBP). Reduction: ATP and
NADPH convert 3-PGA into G3P. Regeneration: some G3P rebuilds RuBP at the cost
of more ATP. Two G3P molecules combine to form one glucose.

## Cellular respiration (overview)
Cellular respiration breaks down glucose to release ATP. Overall equation:
C6H12O6 + 6O2 -> 6CO2 + 6H2O + ATP (about 30-32 ATP per glucose in eukaryotes).
It occurs in three stages: glycolysis (cytoplasm), the Krebs cycle (mitochondrial
matrix), and oxidative phosphorylation (inner mitochondrial membrane).

## Glycolysis
Glycolysis splits one glucose (6 carbons) into two pyruvate (3 carbons each) in
the cytoplasm. It nets 2 ATP and 2 NADH, and requires no oxygen, so it also runs
during anaerobic conditions. When oxygen is absent, pyruvate is fermented into
lactate (animals) or ethanol + CO2 (yeast).

## Krebs cycle (citric acid cycle)
Pyruvate is first oxidised to acetyl-CoA, which enters the Krebs cycle in the
mitochondrial matrix. Each turn releases 2 CO2, and produces 3 NADH, 1 FADH2,
and 1 GTP (≈ATP). The NADH and FADH2 carry electrons to the electron transport
chain. Oxygen is NOT directly consumed here — it acts as the final electron
acceptor later in oxidative phosphorylation.

## Oxidative phosphorylation
On the inner mitochondrial membrane, the electron transport chain passes
electrons from NADH/FADH2 to oxygen, pumping protons into the intermembrane
space. ATP synthase lets protons flow back, making ~26-28 ATP (chemiosmosis).
Cyanide and carbon monoxide are poisons because they block the chain; without
oxygen as the final acceptor, the chain backs up and ATP production collapses.
